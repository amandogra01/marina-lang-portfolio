Companies with an international footprint often face the question of which standard to
report under — or how to reconcile financial statements between HGB, IFRS and US-GAAP.
Here's a short overview of the key differences.

## Purpose of the standards

HGB is primarily creditor-protection oriented and shaped by the prudence principle. IFRS
and US-GAAP, by contrast, aim first and foremost to give investors a realistic view of a
company's financial position and performance ("true and fair view" / "fair presentation").

## Measurement differences

- **Fixed assets:** HGB holds to historical cost, while IFRS permits revaluation to fair
  value in certain cases.
- **Provisions:** IFRS and US-GAAP often require discounting future obligations, where HGB
  applies more simplified rules.
- **Revenue recognition:** IFRS 15 and its US-GAAP counterpart (ASC 606) follow a unified
  five-step model that has no direct equivalent under HGB.

## Why it matters in practice

For consolidated financial statements, or when dealing with US or international investors,
a clean reconciliation between standards is essential. Understanding the conceptual
differences early avoids surprises during audit and consolidation.
