An ERP implementation in the finance function is more than an IT project — it changes
processes, roles and controls across the whole company. Having supported several system
migrations (including Navision to SAP R/3, IDEAL to Sage, and Diamant to AlphaPlan), a
few recurring success factors stand out.

## Business requirements come first

Before a system is configured, the business requirements from accounting and controlling
need to be clearly defined: chart of accounts, close processes, reporting structures, and
regulatory requirements (HGB, IFRS, US-GAAP). Skipping this step leads to costly rework
later on.

## Concept review as the connecting link

Reviewing the functional concept ensures that the solution proposed by IT and
implementation partners actually fits the financial processes. Close collaboration between
finance and IT pays off especially here.

## User acceptance testing and go-live

Before go-live, real business scenarios should be tested end-to-end — including
month-end close scenarios. Structured user acceptance testing significantly reduces the
risk of process disruption after cutover.

## Conclusion

A successful ERP implementation in finance depends on early involvement of the business
function, clear requirements, and a careful testing phase — not on the technology alone.
